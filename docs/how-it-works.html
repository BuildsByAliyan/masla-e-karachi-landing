Meet Ahmed

An example story

Ahmed is an imaginary person. His story shows how the platform is meant to work once it is live. The platform is still under development.

  1. Day 1

    Ahmed sees an open gutter cover

    Ahmed is 32 and lives in Gulshan-e-Iqbal, Block 13-D. On his way home he sees a gutter with no cover.

    A neighbour shares the website link on WhatsApp. Ahmed reports the problem in four quick steps. He does not type his name, phone number or email.

    At the end he receives a secret tracking code to follow his report.

    Your secret tracking code MEK-8F3K-92
    Open
  2. Day 1 to 2

    A moderator checks the report

    A moderator reviews the photo and the location. Faces and number plates are blurred.

    Once approved, the problem appears on the public map as a red pin, with the responsible department named: KWSC.

    Under review
  3. Day 3

    The department replies

    Neighbours open the report and press “I am affected too”. The verified KWSC officer answers from the official dashboard, fixes the gutter and uploads an after-photo.

    Everyone can see that the department responded.

    Responded
  4. Day 5

    The community confirms

    Residents nearby are asked if it is really fixed. They confirm, and the pin turns green.

    The story appears in Success Stories, and the department’s record improves because the fix is real.

    Resolved

What the pin colours mean

Only four colours, always used in the same way.

Open

The problem is public and is waiting for the department.

Under review

A moderator is checking the report before it goes public.

Responded

The department has replied and work is going on.

Resolved

It is fixed, with proof, and the community has confirmed it.

How accountability works

Three simple rules keep the system fair.

Seven days, then Overdue

If a department does nothing for 7 days, the problem gets an Overdue flag.

A senior officer is emailed and the department’s ranking goes down. Everyone can see it.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7
Open After 7 days with no action Overdue

Citizens can fix things too

Sometimes a neighbour fixes the problem. Any user can press “This Problem Is Fixed” and upload an after-photo.

The photo’s location must match the original. A moderator checks it, nearby residents vote, and then it is marked as resolved by the community. Department rankings only count work the department really did.

Rankings use data, not likes

Departments are ranked on what they actually did. Popularity and likes are never part of the ranking.

What we do and what we do not

We are honest about our limits.

What we never do

  • Force a department to act: our power is public transparency
  • Take sides in politics or accuse any person
  • Ask for your name, phone number or email
  • Work as an emergency service

What we do

  • Let anyone report without giving a name
  • Make every problem and every reply public
  • Rank departments on real data
  • Ask the community to confirm fixes
